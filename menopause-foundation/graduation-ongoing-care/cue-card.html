Graduation visit — presenter cues

Print & keep at desk · never on screen
Standing rule 1The offer is information, never pressure.
Standing rule 2Bad visit = slow down. Never speed up to the offer. Slide 14 (questions) can be skipped if she's ready.
  1. CongratulationsGive it a beat. Reference one thing from her journey before advancing, in her words.
  2. None of this was luckLet the receipt print and stamp, then point at it: "Six steps, every one done. That's not luck."
  3. The moving targetLet the 3-D model tour before → after → treatment. One line: "This is why the plan keeps adjusting."
  4. After week 12Read the left column first, then the right. Plain and neutral: "Here's the difference a plan makes." She can still book single visits, so say so.
  5. Your next twelve monthsIt advances by itself. Click a stop to hold it. Transition visit, then an NP visit and body scan every 3 months. Labs only when her NP recommends them.
  6. Care that keeps upFour promises; the phone follows along. Click a row to hold it while you talk. Say "wherever possible" about the NP, as written. The screens are illustrative.
  7. When something changesPick the one or two that match her. Don't read all six.
  8. The transition visit30 minutes with the care coordinator, within 2 weeks of enrolling. NP visits for the year get booked here.
  9. Body scanThe same InBody she had in Foundation. Keep it short.
  10. Member eveningsOptional. The three topics are examples, and topics and speakers change.
  11. Your choice"Here's what both look like." Neutral voice; the layout steers.
  12. PricingVerbatim: "The same care on either plan. Yearly is $995, which works out to $83 a month. Monthly is $99 with a 3-month minimum." Yearly renews automatically: say so out loud. Stop talking after the price. If asked: single NP visits are $325 with no scans, messaging or events; NP receipts may be claimable on extended health plans or a health spending account.
  13. Your plan, plainlyPoint to renewal, cancelling and refunds. Say this, it's no longer on a slide: this is hormone care, not primary care, so she keeps her family physician or walk-in clinic, and urgent symptoms mean 911 or the ED (agreement §1, §8). "Your agreement has the full terms."
  14. Your questionsOnly if she has questions. Otherwise move on.
  15. Keep your momentumChoose a plan, review and sign the agreement, transition visit within 2 weeks. If she's unsure, offer the free 15-minute talk with the care coordinator.
  16. Thank youWarm regardless of her decision.
