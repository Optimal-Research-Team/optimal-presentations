How it works

Oxygen, under pressure.

Inside a pressurized chamber, you breathe oxygen at a concentration and pressure far above normal air. Researchers describe the underlying idea as the “hyperoxic-hypoxic paradox” — repeated, controlled swings in oxygen that the body responds to as a signal.

01

Pressurized chamber

You rest in a clear chamber while pressure is gently raised — similar to the sensation of a plane’s descent. Sessions are calm and monitored throughout.

02

Elevated oxygen

Higher pressure lets far more oxygen dissolve into the blood and plasma than breathing ordinary air ever could, reaching tissues throughout the body.

03

Repeated sessions

The protocol is a series of sessions over weeks. In studies, it’s the repeated cycling — not any single session — that is associated with the biological changes.

The protocol we follow

Inside the protocol.

Our program is modelled on the structured protocol used in the published research on HBOT and aging-associated biomarkers. Rather than continuous oxygen, the protocol uses intermittent oxygen breathing within each session — the pattern researchers associate with the “hyperoxic-hypoxic paradox.”

What the research explores  ·  1 of 2

Potential longevity-related benefits.

A growing body of peer-reviewed research explores how HBOT interacts with the biology of aging. Here’s what studies have observed across four areas — described plainly, with every source cited at the end.

Cellular aging markers

In trials, HBOT has been associated with longer telomeres and fewer senescent immune cells in the blood of aging adults, alongside signals of active DNA repair.

Early biomarker findings — not yet proven to change how we age.

Tissue & regeneration

Studies point to increased blood-vessel growth, shifts in skin collagen density, and better tissue oxygenation and wound-healing dynamics.

Reparative-biology signals, still being studied in healthy people.

What the research explores  ·  2 of 2

Potential longevity-related benefits.

A growing body of peer-reviewed research explores how HBOT interacts with the biology of aging. Here’s what studies have observed across four areas — described plainly, with every source cited at the end.

Brain & neuroplasticity

In injury and impairment groups, HBOT has been linked to changes in cerebral blood flow and gains on some attention and executive-function measures.

Studied mainly after injury — not yet in healthy aging.

Mitochondria & oxidative signalling

Oxygen cycling appears to nudge mitochondrial signalling and the body’s own antioxidant defenses through a hormesis — a beneficial small-stress adaptation — effect.

A plausible mechanism; the longevity link isn’t established.

The bottom line

These are early findings at the level of biology. A longer lifespan, disease prevention in healthy people, and reversal of aging haven’t been proven as clinical outcomes — larger trials are ongoing.

We think the science is genuinely promising, and we’d rather you see exactly where it stands.

In plain terms

Where this fits, plainly.

Hyperbaric oxygen therapy (HBOT) is a well-established treatment for a specific set of medical conditions. Its use for longevity, healthy aging and wellness is newer. That use is still being researched. Health Canada has not approved it as a treatment for aging or disease. We offer it on that basis.

This is a wellness service, not a covered medical treatment. It is not billed to OHIP. Most members pay privately. Some employer HSA plans may apply. Every claim in this presentation cites its source.

What to expect

What a session is like.

HBOT is generally well tolerated. Every program begins with a medical screening, and the physician reviews your history before any session is booked.

Before

Arrival & screening

Wear comfortable cotton clothing. You’ll be checked for anything that shouldn’t go into the chamber and briefed on how to equalize ear pressure.

In the chamber

During the session

You rest, read, or listen to music while pressure is raised slowly. Most people feel only a fullness in the ears, like a flight or a dive.

Good to know

Common, mild effects

Temporary ear or sinus pressure and lightheadedness are the most common. Less common effects, including temporary changes in near vision, are discussed on your call.

After

Afterward

There’s no downtime — you return to your day as normal. Your provider tracks how you tolerate each session across the series.

The evidence

What the research shows.

Most of the research rests on one mechanism, the hyperoxic-hypoxic paradox. Breathing oxygen under pressure and then returning to ordinary air, over and over, appears to trigger the same repair signals the body uses when oxygen runs short, without the shortage itself. Hadanny and Efrati set that mechanism out in Biomolecules in 2020.

Frequently asked

Your questions, answered.

Is HBOT approved by Health Canada for longevity or anti-aging?

No. HBOT is approved by Health Canada for a defined set of medical conditions. Its use for aging, longevity, or general wellness is investigational and is not approved by Health Canada as a treatment for aging or disease prevention. We offer it as a private wellness service on that basis.

Is this covered by OHIP or my insurance?

Not for this indication. Because it’s offered as a longevity and wellness service — not an OHIP-eligible medical treatment — HBOT here is paid privately. Some employer Health Spending Accounts (HSA) may apply; we can discuss this on your info call.

Who administers the treatment, and where?

Sessions are delivered at our partner hyperbaric clinic and are administered under the supervision of the physician (MD) who runs that facility. Optimal coordinates your referral to our partner site for physician-guided assessment and treatment.

Will this make me live longer or reverse aging?

We can’t say that, and we won’t. Research has observed changes in some aging-associated biomarkers, but lifespan extension, disease prevention in healthy people, and reversal of biological aging have not been demonstrated as clinical outcomes.
