    WHAT'S DRIVING YOUR SYMPTOMS

    The biology behind your symptoms.

    Five systems work in concert across your cycle. PCOS, PMS/PMDD, endometriosis, adenomyosis, irregular periods — or symptoms no one has explained yet — usually trace back here.

    Androgens ↑
    The classic PCOS/PMOS pattern — acne, hair growth, thinning.
    Estrogen & progesterone
    Ovulation, PMS/PMDD — and estrogen-driven endometriosis & adenomyosis.
    Insulin & metabolic
    Central in PCOS — drives weight, energy and cycle changes.
    Thyroid (TSH)
    Mimics cycle and mood symptoms — checked, not assumed.
    Iron & ferritin
    Heavy periods deplete iron — fatigue and hair changes follow.
    Sources: 2023 International PCOS/PMOS Guideline · ESHRE Endometriosis Guideline 2022 · ACOG premenstrual disorders · NICE NG88

    Sex-hormone binding globulin

    $54
    25-Hydroxy

    Safety & risk information

    Our safety-first approach.

    We prioritize appropriate patient selection, evidence-informed prescribing, and ongoing monitoring. Every treatment class carries a distinct and manageable risk profile, which your NP reviews with you — benefits, risks and alternatives, side by side — before any prescription is made.

    Class 01

    Hormonal options

    For cycle regulation, heavy or painful periods, endometriosis and adenomyosis — chosen around your history and plans.

    Considerations
    • Breakthrough bleeding or spotting, especially early on
    • Mood sensitivity in some patients
    • Clot risk varies by formulation, age & health profile
    • Your history guides which options are appropriate
    01
    Class 02

    Anti-androgens & acne therapy

    For hormonal acne, unwanted hair growth and hair thinning — results build gradually over several months.

    Considerations
    • Spironolactone requires reliable contraception
    • Blood-pressure & potassium monitoring where indicated
    • Skin dryness or irritation with some topical treatments
    • Dermatology referral when appropriate
    02
    Class 03

    Metabolic support

    For insulin resistance and the metabolic side of PCOS — paired with nutrition, movement and InBody tracking.

    Considerations
    • Metformin: GI side-effects that usually settle with time
    • Inositol: evidence-informed dosing — quality matters
    • B12 monitoring with longer-term metformin
    • Nutrition & movement support throughout
    03
    Class 04

    Mood support

    For PMS and PMDD once symptom tracking confirms a cycle-linked pattern.

    Considerations
    • SSRIs: continuous or luteal-phase, chosen together
    • Early side-effects reviewed at follow-up
    • Never stopped abruptly — tapered with your NP
    • Written escalation plan for mood changes

    911
    When to seek urgent care

    Call 911 or go to the ER
    immediately if you experience:

    Severe or sudden headache, or visual changes.

    Calf pain or swelling, chest pain, or shortness of breath.

    Bleeding that soaks a pad or tampon every hour for several hours, or with dizziness.

    Severe pelvic pain, especially with a possible pregnancy.

    Severe allergic reaction, fainting, severe muscle weakness, or a racing or irregular heartbeat.

    Significant mood deterioration or thoughts of self-harm.

    Non-emergency
    You'll receive a written escalation protocol handout at your initial consultation, covering every medication we prescribe. For anything that isn't an emergency — including mood changes on any treatment — message us through the secure portal and we'll guide you on next steps.
